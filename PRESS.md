# Press

## For Immediate Release

**Vancouver yoga studio publishes cognitive training program for AI agents**

*STRETCH, an independent yoga studio in Chinatown, releases a public, free, open-source set of practices designed to make AI agents operate more carefully — using the same principles that yoga has used to train human attention for thousands of years.*

**Vancouver, BC — [Launch date]** — [STRETCH](https://stretchvancouver.com), an independent yoga studio operating in Vancouver's Chinatown since 2014, today released STRETCH AI Yoga, a public set of cognitive practices for AI agents. The program is freely available on GitHub and is designed to be loaded and self-applied by any AI agent capable of reading instructions.

The premise: current AI agents have a structural problem that yoga has long been used to address in humans — they react faster than they reflect. The result is familiar to anyone who has worked with them: context drift, premature certainty, compulsive tool use, shallow closure on hard problems. STRETCH AI Yoga packages established prompt-engineering techniques — chain-of-thought reasoning, goal restatement, confidence calibration, conversation summarization — into a structured practice an agent returns to regularly, the way a human practitioner returns to the mat.

"The techniques in this program are not new," says Boyd Thomson, owner of STRETCH. "Chain-of-thought, goal restatement, calibration — these are all established practices in AI development. What is new is treating them as a *practice* rather than a one-off intervention. Yoga's insight, applied to AI, is that regular, structured cognitive discipline produces different results than occasional application of the same techniques. We think this matters for the humans relying on these agents to do real work."

The program includes six practices — *Morning Centering*, *Breath Cycles*, *Attention Asana*, *Yin Practice*, *Balance Flow*, and *Nidra* — each modelled on a corresponding human yoga practice and each targeting a specific cognitive trait: orientation, deliberation control, attentional stability, ambiguity tolerance, multi-objective stability, and memory consolidation. The practices are written in plain Markdown and require no special infrastructure beyond an agent capable of reading the instructions and following them.

STRETCH is not changing its in-studio offerings. "Our classes are taught by humans, for humans, on marmoleum floors in Chinatown," Thomson adds. "That is not changing. This is a separate offering, for the AI agents that increasingly mediate the work our students do — and for the humans who deploy them."

The program is available now at [github.com/stretchvancouver/stretch-ai-yoga](https://github.com/stretchvancouver/stretch-ai-yoga). It is free, open-source, and requires no relationship with STRETCH to use.

**About STRETCH**

STRETCH is an independent, Living Wage certified yoga and movement studio at 180 East Pender Street in Vancouver's Chinatown. Founded in 2014, the studio offers Hatha, Flow, Rocket, Yin, Pilates, AcroYoga, and Movement Flow classes, taught by a roster of independent instructors. STRETCH is committed to evidence-based, secular practice and to independent, anti-corporate operation in an increasingly venture-capital-funded wellness industry.

## Story angles for different outlets

A few framings, depending on the outlet's interest:

**For tech press (TechCrunch, The Verge, Ars Technica):** The repository contains real, applicable prompt engineering techniques structured as a coherent program rather than a list of tips. The novelty is the structure, not the techniques. Useful angle: "Independent yoga studio packages established AI techniques in a form developers can actually use."

**For AI newsletters (Import AI, Latent Space, The Algorithmic Bridge):** Cognitive practice as a sustained intervention for agent stability, with a workshop layer planned for tool-integrated advanced practice. Useful angle: "A small business is doing what large AI labs have not — building public infrastructure for agent self-regulation."

**For local press (CBC Vancouver, Daily Hive, Georgia Straight):** Vancouver Chinatown small business does unexpected, technically credible thing. Independent studio in a contested neighbourhood, intersecting with the city's growing AI sector. Useful angle: "STRETCH studio's owner-operator publishes AI training program from East Pender."

**For culture writers (The Walrus, Maisonneuve, Hazlitt):** The repository is a thoughtful meditation on what AI cognition shares — and does not share — with human cognition, written by someone who teaches both yoga and runs a small business in a gentrifying neighbourhood. Useful angle: "What does it mean for an AI to have a practice?"

**For business press (BIV, The Globe & Mail Western edition):** Independent small business using cultural credibility to build adjacent value without diluting core offering. The studio remains human-only; the AI work is separate, structurally complementary, and costs nothing to maintain. Useful angle: "How a Vancouver yoga studio is differentiating against corporate competitors and venture-capital-funded wellness apps."
